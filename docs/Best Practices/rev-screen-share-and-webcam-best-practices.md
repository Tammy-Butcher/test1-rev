Rev screen share tools use technology (WebRTC) built into current, modern browsers. If you do not see the features, or they cannot initiate captures, please review the brand and version of your browser for WebRTC support.

These tools are supported on PCs and Macs. Mobile devices can utilize these tools, but only with the webcam (meaning, there is no screenshare).  

This topic details the best practices to use when using Rev features such as [Screen Share and Record](doc:screen-share-and-record), [Producer Events](doc:produce-an-event), and [Presenting a Webcam and Screenshare Event](doc:host-a-self-produced-webcast).

## Equipment Best Practices

1. Do not to move the webcam around during recordings. Use a good quality tripod for the camera if possible.
2. Get an external microphone and external webcam if possible. Make sure they are stationary.
3. Do not refresh the browser window. Refreshing the browser window causes disruptions within the stream. It will be necessary to **STOP** and **RESTART** the broadcast/event in this case.
4. Do not modify your screen resolution in the middle of your presentation.
5. When switching currently shared browser tab content to a second browser tab, first stop sharing the first browser tab.  After stopping, you can then start sharing the second browser tab.  (This avoids a known issue with browser tab sharing and audio.)
6. If possible, please use a wired computer, as opposed to wireless -- with a strong internet connection.
7. Always try to use the most up-to-date browser version.
8. Don't forget to disable any application that might popup/interrupt your presentation.
9. The forgotten mistake -- enable your microphone -- **Always test your audio.** 

> 🚧 Important!
>
> The computer you use should be modern. Consider the current **minimum requirements**: Sixth Generation Intel Processor with 4 Cores (or comparable AMD), 16GB RAM, Enabled Hardware Acceleration. Please shut down unnecessary applications to aid CPU and memory efficiency.
>
> Reminder: Your microphone must always be enabled with permissions (for the browser and URL). If you do not want to record/stream audio, please mute it.

> 👍 Tip
>
> Producer uses multiple devices and inputs from various sources including your microphone, camera, and sometimes live screen sharing. To achieve the best stream possible, it is highly recommended you close all other applications during a Producer event so that all system resources are dedicated to the webcast.

## Environment Best Practices

1. Eliminate background noise.
2. If you are recording your screen with audio, then also eliminate background computer applications. Also, disable any that would interfere -- **pop-ups destroy recordings**.
3. Turn off your cell phone.  Or, at a minimum, disable notifications and calls for the duration of the event.  If you utilize vibrate, please place your phone away from the microphone.
4. Check your light levels, quality and direction (Is your face in the shadows? When you look up or down, does that introduce shadows?) Consider a light kit.
5. Dress appropriately for webcams. **Avoid clothes that have small patterns, saturated reds, blacks or whites**. (Suggestions: solid, muted colors that will modestly contrast with your background.)

## Presenting Best Practices

1. Plan your presentation. Know what you want your viewers to take away from the presentation, and plan for it. Know your audience, and give them a reason to care about what you are saying.
2. Script out what you are going to say. Design an opening that grips them, and an ending that completes them. Use it as a reference, and resist the urge to "just read it"! Consider a teleprompter (there are many software-based ones available -- but don't record them!).
3. If using materials, consider getting graphic-design help. At a minimum, have your materials reviewed.
4. More stories.  More graphics.  Less reading.
5. If you are using a webcam, keep in mind the direction you are looking. If you are looking at your monitor, you are not looking directly into the camera which means your are not making eye contact with the viewers. Eye contact can be difficult, so practice.
6. Practice/Rehearse! Run through your presentation/recording at least once--and read it out loud! Then again and record it. Watch it. Rinse and Repeat.
7. Focus on content and simplicity. Tell them what you are going to say. Say it. Tell them what you said.
8. Record and review any shared content for legibility and readability.
9. Lean slightly forward during the recording.  Use your hands.  Slow down.
10. If recording, remember you can "fix it in 'post'" by editing it out. So, pause, take/release a deep breath, smile, and then restart scene. (Don't forget to remove it in 'post'!) Remember to just "roll with it" -- "the show must go on!"
