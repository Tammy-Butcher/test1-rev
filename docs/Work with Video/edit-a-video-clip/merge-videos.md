You can use the **Clips** icon to to insert and combine multiple videos anywhere on the timeline to create a single seamless video sequence. You can add bumpers and trailers, combine multiple videos into a highlight reel, and then publish them without ever having to download the file.

When you have finished merging the clips on the video timeline, you can then save your video as an entirely new video that is different from the original you started with.  

> 📘 Note
> 
> As a reminder, Rev video conference integration recordings contain _both_ speaker and content and, as a result, are stored as dual (or two) stream videos.  Other recordings and uploaded videos in Rev are stored as single stream videos.
> 
> When merging videos in the Rev Editor, each video _must_ be of the same type. This means:
> 
> - You can only merge dual-stream videos (VCI recordings) with other dual-stream videos
> - You can only merge single-stream videos with other single-stream videos
> - In other words, you may not "mix" video stream types when you merge them
> 
> Additionally, when _searching_ for videos to merge, you are only presented with the same stream videos as the video you are currently editing.  You will not, for instance, see dual-stream videos in the search selection if you are editing a single stream video.
> 
> This is only a short term limitation. A future release will support "mixed" editing for different types.

Video clips can be selected and then merged on your timeline using the **Clips** icon using a variety of methods. 

For example, you can:

- Choose from **All Videos** or select a clip from just your own videos using **My Videos**
- Search for a specific video to merge using a **keyword search**
- Insert pre-roll and post-roll (tops and tails) videos on the timeline
- Insert a video in the middle of the timeline (or anywhere you like) by defining slices first

> 🚧 Important!
> 
> If you modify the original video timeline in any way by merging it with other videos, your closed captioning and/or subtitles will no longer align and you will need to re-do them.  Vbrick is working on a future solution to this issue.

Inserting the video places it at the _beginning_ or _end_ of the timeline based on where the playback head is positioned at 50% when the button is clicked. If you drag and drop it, it will appear either at the beginning or end of the video or in the slice you have identified.  Both methods are discussed below.

## Merge Bumper and Trailer Clips on Your Timeline

If you have not [defined a slice](doc:add-slices-to-a-video) in your video, the clip you choose to insert and merge is automatically placed at either the _beginning_ or _end_ of your timeline depending on how close the playback head is to either.

The same is true if you drag and drop the clip in that you can either drag it to the beginning or end of the video and, as demonstrated in the image above, the area it will be placed, is highlighted in green.

If you prefer instead to merge a video clip in the middle of your timeline (or anywhere other than the beginning or end), you must first [add a slice](doc:add-slices-to-a-video) where it can be placed. This is detailed below.

> 👍 Tip
> 
> You can [trim inserted clips](doc:add-slices-to-a-video#trim-a-video-clip) as needed once merged.

Keep in mind that you can also choose to have the bumper and trailer clips inserted into your recordings automatically during event set-up. For example, you can choose to have your company's logo as the bumper and contact information as the trailer to make editing easier once the event is concluded.

## Merge a Video Clip in the Middle of Your Timeline with Slices

When you [add a slice](doc:add-slices-to-a-video) to a video you can now decide to merge your video at the beginning or end of the slice by clicking insert or dragging and dropping it instead of at the beginning or end of the video itself.

Use the **Save** icon to save the edited version of the video or to cancel all edits (if not saved first) without saving changes. You can also select **Save As New** which saves the video as a new video. Keep in mind that this then treats the video as a new video and goes through any approval processes if necessary.
